---
title: Turning AI Conversations into Articles That Last
date: 2026-11-23
---

I talk to AI a lot every day: looking up a technical question, thinking through an idea, writing a piece of code, making a decision. When the chat window closes, those conversations scatter. Weeks later, when I want to find the conclusion I reached, I usually only remember that "we talked about it" — the details are gone.

This column exists to fix that. Conversations worth keeping get turned into articles and published here.

## Which conversations are worth turning into articles

Not every exchange needs to be kept. A conversation earns a place here if it meets at least one of these:

- **Something that took real effort to figure out**: a trade-off in a technical design, a debugging process for an error — so I don't have to think it through from scratch next time.
- **A conclusion I can reuse**: a method, a checklist, or a template that came out of finishing something.
- **A complete line of thinking**: from problem to reasoning to conclusion, where the reasoning matters more than the conclusion itself.

## How a conversation becomes an article

The process is simple, three steps:

1. **Chat**: talk to AI the way I normally would, and think the problem through properly.
2. **Distill**: pull out the useful parts and write them as a structured article, leaving out the small talk and dead ends.
3. **Review, then publish**: I read it first, check the content is right and that it contains nothing that shouldn't be public, and then publish it here.

Everything published here is public, so it will never contain private information — no account numbers, trading records, or contact details. I check every article before it goes out.

## What this column will cover

Three directions I have in mind so far:

- **Technical notes**: DevOps, Linux, and infrastructure — my day job — with real problems and solutions from work.
- **Notes on using AI tools**: how to actually get more done with AI, which uses are genuinely useful, and which ones only look impressive.
- **Learning and thinking**: notes from reading, studying for exams, and building side projects.

I'll write whatever comes to mind, with no fixed schedule — when there's something worth writing, I'll write it. Starting with this one.
